# Epistemology and Explanatory Knowledge

## 1. Hard-to-Vary Explanations

A good explanation is hard to vary. Its details cannot be adjusted after the fact without ruining its ability to account for reality.

Theories that easily bend to accommodate contradictory observations are myths or pseudo-science. In business and strategy, reject flexible post-hoc narratives. Demand clear, testable, and falsifiable hypotheses.

## 2. Wealth as Knowledge

Matter and energy are abundant and conserved throughout the universe. Scarcity is epistemological, not physical.

All wealth comes from transforming and rearranging matter and energy according to explanatory knowledge. Problems are inevitable, but all problems are solvable given sufficient knowledge.

## 3. Fallibilism and Error Correction

All knowledge is conjectural and subject to error. No authoritative source of truth exists.

Effective organizations avoid bureaucratic error prevention, which breeds paralysis. Instead, optimize for fast error detection, feedback loops, and rapid correction.

## 4. Reading and Foundations

- Read what you love until you love to read. Habit precedes discernment.
- Read original sources (Adam Smith, Charles Darwin, Richard Feynman) rather than secondary commentary.
- Five foundational skills: reading, writing, arithmetic, persuasion, and programming.
- Prioritize microeconomics, game theory, logic, and mathematics over transient trade books.
