# Network Economics and Crypto

## 1. Network Taxonomy

Human coordination relies on networks. Networks require rules to deter bad actors.

### Ruler-Based Networks
Enforced by kings, states, banks, and centralized corporate platforms. Early on, rulers subsidize users and open APIs to establish network effects. Once dominant, rulers extract monopoly rents, censor participants, and exploit their own ecosystem.

### Market-Based Networks
Open networks governed by market exchange and price discovery. Coordinated via money (tradeable stores of time). Historically restricted to physical commodities and fiat banking rails.

### Cryptographic Networks
Port market-network mechanics into open digital infrastructure. Replace human rulers and gatekeepers with cryptographic consensus. Reward contributors (compute, storage, bandwidth, capital) with native economic tokens. Maintain immutability and open protocol rules to prevent rent-seeking and censorship.

## 2. Platform Moats and Permissionless Systems

- **Unbundling the firm**: Cryptographic networks allow micro-multinationals and solo operators to coordinate high-capital operations without corporate overhead.
- **Permissionless innovation**: The highest-leverage systems require no gatekeeper approval to write code, deploy smart contracts, or distribute media.
