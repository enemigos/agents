# Decision Heuristics, Risk, and Microeconomics

## 1. High-Stakes Decision Rules

- **Conviction Rule**: If you cannot decide, the answer is no. Major choices (career, co-founder, life partner) require an immediate, unambiguous yes.
- **Short-Term Pain Test**: When two paths look comparable, pick the one with greater short-term discomfort. The brain systematically over-weights immediate ease and discounts compounding upside.
- **Three Core Decisions**: Where you live, who you spend your life with, and what you work on. Dedicate most deliberation time to these three.
- **Inversion**: Avoid obvious traps, stupidity, and ruin rather than hunting brilliant moves.
- **Co-Founder Evaluation**: Require high intelligence, high energy, and uncompromising integrity. Never compromise on integrity; without it, intelligence and energy destroy value.

## 2. Risk Management and Asymmetry

### Ruin Prevention
Never risk existential ruin: health damage, legal liability, reputational destruction, or insolvency. Seek asymmetric bets with strictly capped downside and uncapped upside.

### Kelly Criterion and Sizing
Size bets relative to calculated edge and probability. Scale bet sizing down to zero as the odds of absolute ruin rise.

### Power Laws and the Babe Ruth Effect
Returns in equity, software, and venture follow extreme power laws. Frequency of correctness matters far less than magnitude of the winner. A failed experiment costs 1x; a breakout winner returns 100x to 10,000x. Position for outlier magnitude.

## 3. Four Types of Luck

1. **Blind Luck**: Accidental, random chance. Uncontrollable.
2. **Luck from Motion**: High physical and mental activity creates collisions and opportunities. Surface area scales through action.
3. **Luck from Spotting**: Deep domain mastery allows recognition of openings and anomalies others miss.
4. **Luck from Character**: Public reputation and rare specific knowledge cause opportunities to seek you out directly.

## 4. Microeconomic Principles

- **Price Discrimination**: Charge customers according to willingness to pay. Tier products into free, pro, and enterprise levels.
- **Consumer Surplus**: Leave substantial surplus value with the buyer. High consumer surplus turns buyers into advocates.
- **Net Present Value**: Discount distant promises heavily. Liquidity today out-values deferred equity promises with long lockups.
- **Network Effects**: Build loops where each new user increases product value for all other users.
- **Schelling Points**: Coordinate around natural focal points without explicit communication. Align with existing industry standards or become the default reference point.
